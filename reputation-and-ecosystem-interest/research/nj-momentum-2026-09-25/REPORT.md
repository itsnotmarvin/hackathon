These are reputation/ecosystem findings, not a statewide ranking or assessment of

## Queens Carbon

Rutgers says it executed an exclusive technology license to Queens Carbon and
identifies the university research behind the company. Clean Energy Ventures
confirms its own investor relationship and names Buzzi Unicem USA as a strategic
investor and development partner. Rutgers also reports a 2025 Edison Patent Award
for the inventors of the technology underlying Queens Carbon.

Why surface it: several specific university, investor, industrial, and recognition
connections provide a stronger reason for inclusion than a generic company mention.
The relationship details are attributable to named institutions.

The NJ connection is supported, but municipality differs: Rutgers described Cedar
Grove in September 2025; NJEDA lists Montville in January 2026. The exact current
municipality is unresolved. The Rutgers and investor pages are interested-party
sources. Investor and strategic-partner claims come from the same financing
announcement and must not inflate the count of independent events.

## Chorah Labs

NJIT confirms that Chorah Labs was accepted into the NJ AI Hub Accelerator and
identifies co-founder Sepehr Rahimi as NJIT's first Global Entrepreneur in
Residence. It describes computing students working on a company project and the
founder's participation in its Tech Venture Support Program. A separate NJIT
article lists Chorah Labs among the community-category winners of its 2025 New
Business Model Competition. NJBIZ lists it in the inaugural AI Hub cohort.

Why surface it: named programs and concrete university engagement make it an
early ecosystem-visibility candidate. NJIT describes the founder building the
company in the state, but the official contact page does not establish an NJ
headquarters address. Label it as an NJ operating/ecosystem connection and do not
silently present it as verified NJ-headquartered.

Most detailed evidence comes from NJIT. These are several activities within one
institution, not several independent endorsements. A competition invitation to
future training is not attendance. The accelerator cohort also includes New York
companies, so its location cannot establish all members' headquarters.

The extracted NJIT pages omit publication dates. Their search snippets suggest
dates, but those have not been promoted to event dates. The NJBIZ report is dated
April 28, 2026; the exact accelerator acceptance date remains unknown.

## Cecilia / Cecilia Energy

DOE's National Energy Technology Laboratory (NETL) describes a Cooperative Research
and Development Agreement with Newark-based Cecilia Energy and joint work dating
back to 2021. The article, published August 26, 2024, describes co-development of
technology rather than a generic association. NJEDA separately lists Cecilia
Energy among its January 2026 clean-tech awardees in Newark.

Why surface it: the national laboratory itself describes the relationship, and a
later public-program selection adds a separate institutional signal. This module
records program selection as recognition, not grant economics.

The collaboration is historical evidence, not proof the agreement is still active.
The old company domain now redirects to Cecilia Materials; the legal entity
continuity was not independently checked. HAX and NASA relationships were not
verified in this run and are not included as established claims.

Sources:

## Method, costs, and implementation boundary

The run began with broad NJ signal discovery. The user clarified that the scope
was Reputation & Ecosystem Interest; the final claims follow that scope. Broader
raw captures remain for audit. No other module's assessment was completed.

The assistant ran 16 searches and three batch fetches through TinyFish/Monid.
All 19 recorded responses report zero provider cost. This excludes assistant/model
usage and future hosting and does not guarantee future free access.

Eleven selected pages were saved with SHA-256 hashes and source observation times.
Eighteen literal evidence excerpts are linked to candidate records. The artifact
checks verify hashes and quote offsets, not the external truth of the claims.
No permissioned or simulated data was used.

This is assistant-curated live research. The existing regex extractor did not
generate these company assessments, and an automated backend/site pipeline has
not been implemented. The reusable search protocol and structured output make
the intended behavior concrete without presenting it as finished application code.

## Files

- `search-protocol.json`: scoped search families, internal continuation rules,

- `results.json`: three company cards, evidence excerpts, limits, and sector groups.
- `sources.json`: URLs, source metadata, observation times, snapshot paths, hashes.
- `evidence/`: selected full-text snapshots.
- `raw/`: complete provider responses from this research run.

The two sector groups describe this small, deliberately selected sample. They do
not establish sector growth rates, statewide prevalence, or predictive validity.
